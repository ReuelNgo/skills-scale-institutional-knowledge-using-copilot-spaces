# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This README is the central entry point for OctoAcme's project management documentation. It provides a brief overview of how OctoAcme runs projects and links to every process document in this folder.

## Project Management Processes Summary

OctoAcme follows a lifecycle-based, customer-first, iterative project management approach that moves initiatives through initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective improvement:

- **Initiation** validates the business need, identifies stakeholders, defines a measurable (SMART) outcome, assesses risks and resources, and confirms a go/no-go decision before work begins.
- **Planning** turns an approved initiative into a prioritized, estimated backlog with acceptance criteria, dependencies, milestones, a release plan, and a Definition of Done.
- **Execution and tracking** use a project board (e.g., Backlog, Ready, In Progress, In Review, QA, Done), small pull requests, automated CI quality checks, daily standups, weekly delivery syncs, demos, and progress metrics to keep delivery visible and on track.
- **Risk management and communication** maintain a risk register, provide regular stakeholder updates, and escalate blockers through a defined path — team, Project Manager, Product Lead, and Sponsor — when needed.
- **Release and deployment** require completed acceptance criteria, passing CI and security scans, release notes, a rollback plan, staging smoke tests, production verification, and communication of the release to stakeholders.
- **Retrospectives and continuous improvement** capture learnings after each sprint, release, or incident, and convert them into a small number of owned, time-bound improvements whose impact is tracked.
- **Roles and personas** clarify responsibilities: Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, resources, and communication; Product Managers define vision, priorities, and acceptance criteria; Developers implement, test, and flag technical risks; QA/Testing validates quality and acceptance; and Stakeholders provide input, approvals, and feedback throughout.
